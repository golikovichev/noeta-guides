What a question looks like

One real question from the bank, with the answer and the document it was written from.

According to the NHS, one unit of alcohol is equivalent to how much pure alcohol?

  1. 10ml or 8g of pure alcohol — correct answer
  2. 25ml or 20g of pure alcohol
  3. 50ml or 40g of pure alcohol
  4. 5ml or 4g of pure alcohol

The NHS states that one unit equals 10ml or 8g of pure alcohol, which is around the amount an average adult can process in an hour.

Source: www.nhs.uk/live-well/alcohol-advice/calculating-alcohol-units/

How to book

Book through an approved centre or an online exam provider — there is no central booking service. The qualification is the mandatory step before applying to your council for the licence itself.

Five more questions, with answers

Every question below is a real question from the bank, written from the document shown with it. The exam does not publish its own questions, so these are not exam questions — they are what the published text supports.

1. In which year was the idea of counting alcohol units first introduced in the UK?

  1. In 1977
  2. In 2007
  3. In 1987 — correct answer
  4. In 1997

The NHS records that the idea of counting alcohol units was first introduced in the UK in 1987 to help people keep track of their drinking.

Source: www.nhs.uk/live-well/alcohol-advice/calculating-alcohol-units/

2. Under section 145 of the Licensing Act 2003, an individual counts as a child for the unaccompanied children offence until reaching which age?

  1. Until they reach the age of 14
  2. Until they reach the age of 16 — correct answer
  3. Until they reach the age of 18, when they become an adult
  4. Until they reach the age of 21

Section 145 defines a child as an individual aged under 16, so the unaccompanied children offence ceases to apply once the young person reaches 16.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/145

3. Section 6 requires each licensing authority to establish a licensing committee. How many members must that committee have?

  1. At least ten but not more than fifteen members — correct answer
  2. At least five but not more than twelve members
  3. At least eight but not more than twenty members
  4. At least twelve but not more than eighteen members

Section 6(1) says each licensing authority must establish a licensing committee of at least ten, but not more than fifteen, members of the authority.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/6

4. Section 139 provides a defence of due diligence, but only for which group of offences?

  1. Carrying on, exposing alcohol for sale, and keeping alcohol for unauthorised sale — correct answer
  2. Selling alcohol to a drunk person, or obtaining alcohol for a drunk person from the premises
  3. Allowing disorderly conduct, and knowingly keeping smuggled goods on premises
  4. Selling alcohol to children, and persistently selling alcohol to children

Section 139(2) applies the defence to the offences in section 136(1)(a), section 137 and section 138: carrying on an unauthorised licensable activity, exposing alcohol for unauthorised sale, and keeping alcohol for unauthorised sale. It does not cover drunk-customer or underage-sales offences.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/139

5. Your pub's premises licence allows the sale of alcohol. The designated premises supervisor has resigned and nobody has taken her place yet. What happens to alcohol sales?

  1. They must stop until a new designated premises supervisor is in post — correct answer
  2. They can continue for up to twenty-eight days while a replacement is found
  3. They can continue as long as a personal licence holder is on duty
  4. They can continue because the licence itself has not been changed

Every premises licence that allows alcohol sales carries this condition. If the post is empty, the licence stops working for alcohol, even though the licence still exists on paper. In practice the licence holder has to name a replacement straight away, or the tills must stop taking alcohol sales.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/19
