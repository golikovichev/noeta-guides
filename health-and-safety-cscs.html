What a question looks like

One real question from the bank, with the answer and the document it was written from.

It is your first day on a new site. What must you be given before you start work?

  1. A site induction — correct answer
  2. A copy of the client contract
  3. A written test on site rules
  4. A tour of the client's offices

The induction is how you learn the risks of this site and how they are controlled. Every worker gets one, and it is about this site, not about building work in general. If nobody gives you one, ask before you start.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/site-rules-induction.htm

How to book

Book online through Pearson VUE or by phone on 0344 994 4488 (CITB booking line). The test is taken at a test centre; the card is applied for afterwards.

Five more questions, with answers

Every question below is a real question from the bank, written from the document shown with it. The exam does not publish its own questions, so these are not exam questions — they are what the published text supports.

1. What does a site have to do about drinking water?

  1. Sell it from a machine in the canteen
  2. Always have it available and clearly marked — correct answer
  3. Provide it only in hot weather
  4. Leave workers to bring their own

Drinking water has to be there all the time, easy to get to and clearly labelled so nobody drinks from the wrong tap. Mains supply is best. If it is stored or bottled it must not be allowed to go stale or get dirty.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/construction/healthrisks/welfare/changing-rooms-and-lockers.htm

2. How should any roof be treated until you know otherwise?

  1. As fragile — correct answer
  2. As safe if it carries a person
  3. As safe if it is less than ten years old
  4. As safe if it is made of metal

Only someone competent can confirm a roof is not fragile, and until they do you stay off it. Do not trust a sheeted roof of any material to hold your weight, including the ridge and the purlins. Rooflights are the worst because paint or poor light hides them.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/roofwork.htm

3. Who must plan a lifting operation involving lifting equipment?

  1. A competent person — correct answer
  2. The crane driver alone
  3. The client's site agent
  4. Whoever ordered the crane

The planner needs real knowledge and experience of the kind of lift being done. The operation then has to be supervised properly and carried out safely. Everyone slinging loads or directing the lift must be trained too.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/lifting-operations.htm

4. Fuel from a site tank has run into a stream. What kind of problem is this?

  1. Water pollution, and it is reported — correct answer
  2. Only a cost problem for the fuel
  3. A matter for the client alone
  4. Nothing, if the stream is small

A spill that reaches a river, stream or canal is water pollution and it is reported. Stop the source if it is safe to do so, contain what you can, then report it. Water carries it far beyond the site.

Source: www.gov.uk/report-environmental-problem

5. Blue smoke is coming from a site generator. What does that usually mean?

  1. The engine is poorly serviced or tuned — correct answer
  2. The engine is running at its best
  3. The fuel tank is nearly empty
  4. The engine has only just been started from cold

Black smoke points to a mechanical fault, and white smoke that clears as the machine warms up is water and unburnt fuel from a cold start. Blue or black is the signal to get the machine seen to, because that exhaust is what people breathe.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/construction/healthrisks/cancer-and-construction/diesel-engine-exhaust.htm
