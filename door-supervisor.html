SIA Door Supervisor

The Level 2 Award for Door Supervisors you need before applying for the licence. Four written papers, sat separately.

Format

Paper 1 — private security industry72 questions, 110 minutes, pass 70%
Paper 2 — working as a door supervisor50 questions, 75 minutes, pass 70%
Paper 3 — conflict management20 questions, 30 minutes, pass 70%
Paper 4 — physical intervention30 questions, 45 minutes, pass 80%, plus a practical assessment

What the exam covers

  • The licence, the law and the worker
  • Licensed premises law, drink and drugs, searching, refusal and ejection
  • Crime scenes, vulnerable people, capacity and queues, arrest
  • Conflict management: recognising, de-escalating, after the incident
  • Physical intervention: reasonable force, restraint risks, reporting

What a question looks like

One real question from the bank, with the answer and the document it was written from.

HSE guidance on work-related violence says something about the people who handle post-incident support. What does it require?

  1. That the venue's insurer appoints a case handler for each incident
  2. That those dealing with the situation are fully trained to support colleagues — correct answer
  3. That only the victim's own manager speaks to them about the incident
  4. That a police officer is present for every such conversation

The guidance asks employers to make sure the workers who deal with post-incident situations are fully trained and know how to support their colleagues, because both victims and witnesses can be affected.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/violence/employer/reporting-learning-from-incidents.htm

How to book

Booking goes through an approved training centre, not a central service: the course and the four papers are arranged by the provider, and you need an emergency first aid qualification before the course starts.

Five more questions, with answers

Every question below is a real question from the bank, written from the document shown with it. The exam does not publish its own questions, so these are not exam questions — they are what the published text supports.

1. A door supervisor is badly shaken after a violent incident and the employer is following HSE guidance. Which support does that guidance mention?

  1. A payment for every incident recorded during that month
  2. A move to a different venue without any discussion
  3. A written warning issued to the attacker by the venue
  4. Confidential counselling or charities such as Victim Support — correct answer

The guidance says an employer can give the worker the details of local counselling services, or of charities such as Victim Support, and can offer its own confidential counselling where it provides one.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/violence/employer/reporting-learning-from-incidents.htm

2. In England and Wales, a man stopped by a constable refuses to give a name and the constable cannot readily find it out. Which reason listed in section 24 of the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 covers arresting him?

  1. To punish him for refusing to answer the constable's questions
  2. To stop him making off before a constable takes over
  3. To enable the name of the person to be ascertained — correct answer
  4. To search his home for evidence of any offence

Section 24(5)(a) lists enabling the person's name to be ascertained where the constable does not know it and cannot readily find it out, or doubts a name already given. The other listed grounds concern preventing harm or loss, protecting vulnerable people and allowing a prompt investigation.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/60/section/24

3. A small bar with three staff is told it must produce a written crowd risk assessment. What does HSE guidance actually say about writing it down?

  1. Below five employees it need not be written down — correct answer
  2. It must be written down and kept for three years
  3. It must be written down and sent to the council
  4. It need not be written down at any size of business

The duty to assess the risks stands whatever the size, but the record in writing starts at five employees.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-assess.htm

4. A venue answers a customer's data request with three pages of legal wording. What style does the UK GDPR require for such information?

  1. Concise, intelligible and in clear plain language — correct answer
  2. The exact wording used in the Regulation itself
  3. A summary written by the venue's solicitor
  4. The format used by the venue's insurer

The duty is sharper still where the information is addressed to a child, and it can be given in writing or electronically.

Source: www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/12

5. A customer with dementia is struggling to answer your question. Why does NHS advice say you should not hurry her?

  1. Because she may feel pressured if you try to speed up her answers — correct answer
  2. Because a slow answer is usually the wrong answer
  3. Because she cannot hear you when the room is busy
  4. Because the law sets a maximum time for each question

The NHS communication guidance says to give the person time to respond, because they may feel pressured if you try to speed up their answers.

Source: www.nhs.uk/conditions/dementia/living-with-dementia/communication/
