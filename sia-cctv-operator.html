What a question looks like

One real question from the bank, with the answer and the document it was written from.

According to the ICO's guidance on automatic number plate recognition (ANPR), what is required of the cameras and of any algorithms used to determine a match?

  1. The cameras and any algorithms used must be approved by the manufacturer before they can lawfully be used on a public road, whatever their accuracy
  2. Both the cameras and any algorithms used to determine a match must be of sufficient quality to prevent misidentification of a vehicle registration mark — correct answer
  3. The cameras must be of sufficient quality, but the algorithms used to determine a match may be supplied by any third party without further checks
  4. The cameras and any algorithms used must be capable of reading a plate at any distance and in any lighting conditions, however poor the conditions are in practice

The ICO's guidance says that the cameras and any algorithms used to determine a match must be of sufficient quality to prevent a vehicle registration mark from being misidentified.

Source: ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/automatic-number-plate-recognition-anpr/

Five more questions, with answers

Every question below is a real question from the bank, written from the document shown with it. The exam does not publish its own questions, so these are not exam questions — they are what the published text supports.

1. According to the ICO's guidance on facial recognition technology (FRT), how does facial recognition equipment work?

  1. Cameras capture the images, and a human operator then compares each face against a watchlist of known individuals by eye, even if the watchlist is out of date
  2. Cameras capture the images, and the software stores a complete photograph of every face it sees instead of a template
  3. Cameras capture the images, and a database of vehicle registration marks is used to identify the person shown in them
  4. Cameras capture the images, and facial recognition software measures and analyses facial features to produce a biometric template — correct answer

The ICO's guidance explains that cameras are used to capture the images, and that facial recognition software then measures and analyses facial features to produce a biometric template.

Source: ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/facial-recognition-technology-frt-and-surveillance/

2. According to the HSE's Management Standards, what does the 'Control' area of work design cover?

  1. the rate of pay for the role, regardless of length of service
  2. the layout of the control room and the type of equipment used
  3. how much say the person has in the way they do their work — correct answer
  4. the number of breaks allowed during each shift, even on a quiet shift

The HSE's Management Standards state that the Control area is about how much say the person has in the way they do their work.

Source: www.hse.gov.uk/stress/standards/overview.htm

3. According to the ICO's guidance on video surveillance, what must be ensured about the method used to disclose footage to a third party?

  1. That every disclosure is made in person, with no electronic transfer at all, and only during normal office hours on weekdays
  2. That any method of disclosing the footage is secure, so it is only seen by the intended recipient and not lost in transit — correct answer
  3. That the footage is always encrypted onto a physical disk and then deleted from every system before it leaves the building
  4. That the recipient signs a printed form confirming that the footage will never be shown to anyone else, ever, for any reason

The ICO guidance says that any method of disclosing footage must be secure, so that the footage is only seen by the intended recipient and is not lost in transit.

Source: ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/governance-post-deployment/

4. The ICO guidance quotes a definition of 'biometric data' from Article 4(14) of the UK GDPR. Which option matches it?

  1. Personal data from technical processing of physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics that confirms unique identification — correct answer
  2. Personal data extracted from a fingerprint scan or a facial image held in a police database, kept for identification purposes
  3. Personal data about a person's physical or mental health, collected and used by an employer, whether or not it is recorded on camera
  4. Personal data taken from a photograph of a person's face and stored on the surveillance system for later use, whatever the purpose of the recording

The ICO guidance defines biometric data as personal data resulting from specific technical processing relating to physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics, where this allows or confirms the unique identification of a person, such as facial images or fingerprint data.

Source: ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/what-does-this-guidance-address/

5. Under Principle 2 of the Surveillance Camera Code of Practice, the user of a surveillance camera system must take into account...

  1. the cost of the equipment and the amount of staff time needed to run the system on a day to day basis
  2. its effect on individuals and their privacy (with regular reviews to ensure the use remains justified) — correct answer
  3. the views of the manufacturer and any local businesses that might be affected by the installation
  4. the effect on people living and working nearby, but only once a complaint about the system has actually been made

Principle 2 places a duty on the user of a surveillance camera system to consider how the system affects individuals and their privacy. It also requires regular reviews so that the continued use of the system stays justified.

Source: www.gov.uk/government/publications/update-to-surveillance-camera-code/amended-surveillance-camera-code-of-practice-accessible-version
